Found on Facebook, Missing on Google: The Customers You Already Earned Are Slipping Away

October 3, 2026 | 5 min read | Derrick D. Patton
  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. Lost Customers

Picture this: a woman at church raves about the braider who did her daughter's hair. You hear the name, pull out your phone, and search for her on Google. Nothing. No website, no listing, no phone number. So you book someone else. That braider just lost a customer she had already earned, and she never even knew it happened.

If you run a Facebook-only business, this post is for you. That includes dinner plate services, braid sellers, mobile barbers, mobile hairdressers, home bakers, lash techs, and anyone else whose business runs on referrals and a Facebook page. Word of mouth is powerful, and it is how most of you got your first customers. But word of mouth has a quiet last step that most people overlook.

1. Word of Mouth Sends People to Google First

When someone gets a recommendation, they rarely book on the spot. They check you out first. They type your name, or "mobile barber near me," into Google to confirm you're real, see your work, and find a way to reach you. The recommendation got them interested. Google is where they decide.

If that search comes up empty, or only shows a hard-to-find Facebook page they can't open without logging in, many of them simply move on. They aren't judging your work. They just couldn't find you fast enough.

2. Facebook Is Not the Same as Being Found

Facebook is a great place to show your work and talk to existing customers. But it was built to keep people inside Facebook, not to help strangers find you on Google. Pages often rank poorly in search, they can be frustrating for people who aren't logged in, and the details customers want (prices, service area, hours, how to book) get buried under posts.

There is also a bigger risk: you don't own that page. Accounts get locked, algorithms change, and a page you've spent years building can lose its reach overnight. A website is the one piece of your online presence that belongs to you.

3. You Already Did the Hard Part

Earning trust is the hard part, and you've done it. Someone vouched for you. That's worth far more than any ad. Losing that customer at the finish line, because they couldn't find a simple page with your name on it, is the most avoidable kind of lost sale.

The fix isn't complicated:
You don't need a huge site. You need a small, clear, professional one that does a few jobs well.

  • Shows up when someone searches your business name or your service plus your area
  • Shows your best work, your prices or price range, and the areas you travel to
  • Makes it easy to call, text, or book with one tap on a phone
  • Loads fast on mobile, since that is where most of your customers are looking
  • Links to your Facebook page, so your social presence and your website work together

4. Mobile Businesses Need This Even More

If you travel to your customers, you don't have a storefront for people to walk past. Your online presence is your storefront. A mobile barber or hairstylist with a clear page that says where you serve, what you charge, and how to book looks established and trustworthy before the customer ever speaks to you. The same goes for a dinner plate service: a menu, a pickup or delivery area, and an easy way to order beat a scrolling feed of old posts every time.

Don't Lose the Customers You Already Earned

Keep your Facebook page. Keep earning referrals. Then make sure that when those referrals go looking for you, there's something to find. A simple website is a small step that protects the customers you've worked hard to win and gives new ones a way to find you too.
